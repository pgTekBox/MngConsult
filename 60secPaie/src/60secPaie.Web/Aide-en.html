60secPaie Help

This guide describes every screen, every field, every rule and every message of 60secPaie, 60Sec's payroll service for Quebec and Ontario. It follows the order of the menu. The words in quotation marks " " are those you see on screen. Use the filter at the top to find a topic, or Ctrl+F to search for a word across the whole page.

Guide version: October 2026. Rates in effect in the software: year 2026, based on the CRA's T4127 guide and Revenu Québec's TP-1015.F guide.

Overview

This guide describes the screens in Quebec terms. For a company whose province of employment is Ontario, the same screens refer to Ontario tax, CPP, EHT and WSIB, with no QPIP and no RL-1: see Ontario payroll. For the other provinces and the territories, see Payroll for the other provinces and territories.

60secPaie calculates the pay of employees who work in Quebec: federal tax, Québec tax, QPP, Employment Insurance, QPIP, and the employer contributions (QPP, EI, QPIP, HSF, CNESST, CNT). From confirmed pay runs, it produces the pay stubs, the direct deposit file, the amount of deductions to remit to both governments, the boxes of the T4 and RL-1 slips, the CNESST statement of wages and the journal entries.

It shares its user accounts and its companies with 60Sec. Employees are also those of 60Sec: they can be created on either side, but their identity (name, address, position) is edited in 60Sec. 60secPaie adds everything that concerns payroll.

The payroll cycle

  1. Set up the company once: pay frequency, RP and RS numbers, HSF and CNESST rates (Settings › Payroll settings).
  2. Set up each employee once: SIN, frequency, rate or salary, TD1 and TP-1015.3, direct deposit (Employees › Payroll settings), then their recurring lines (Pay items).
  3. Run payroll each period, in 4 steps: period, entry, review, confirmation.
  4. Pay the employees: numbered cheques or direct deposit file; send the pay stubs by email.
  5. Remit the deductions by the due dates, in Remittances.
  6. At year end: T4, RL-1 slips, summaries, CNESST statement, in Reports.

What 60secPaie does not do

  • It transmits no payment, neither to employees nor to governments. It prepares the direct deposit file that you hand over to your bank, and it calculates the remittances that you pay yourself.
  • It does not file the slips. It calculates every box; you enter them in Web Forms (CRA) and My Account for businesses (Revenu Québec).
  • It does not handle employees outside Quebec: the province of employment is always QC.
  • It only knows the rates of the years validated by 60Sec in its administration console (2026 today; 2027 is prepared in December). A pay run dated in a year that is not validated is refused.
  • It does not replace your judgement: "Always review your pay runs before confirming them." The calculated amounts remain the employer's responsibility.

Accounts and passwords

Accounts are those of 60Sec. 60secPaie does not create accounts and does not change passwords: "Forgot your password? Reset it from the 60Sec sign-in page." An account deactivated or deleted in 60Sec loses access immediately, even mid-session.

All users who have access to a company have the same rights in 60secPaie. The 60Sec administrator role is read, but no page requires it for now.

Companies and switching company

The list of accessible companies follows the 60Sec rule: an ordinary user sees their company, an accountant sees all those for which they are the accountant. With a single company, its name appears at the top right. With several, a drop-down list ("Switch company") offers them; after a switch, you return to the dashboard.

The current company is the same as in 60Sec (same session key). Its name and address are copied from 60Sec once per session, for the pay stubs, reports and files.

Languages (FR, EN, ES)

The "FR | EN | ES" links in the header, on the sign-in page and on the home page change the language of the whole screen: texts, buttons, tooltips, confirmations, CSV export headers. The choice is remembered in a cookie for one year and survives sign-out. You can also add ?lang=en or ?lang=es to an address.

  • Dates are always in YYYY-MM-DD format, in all three languages.
  • Amounts are written "1 234,56 $" in French, "$1,234.56" in English and Spanish.
  • The pay stub sent by email and its PDF are in the employee's language (chosen on their record). The PDF downloaded from the screen is in the user's language.
  • This guide is available in French, English and Spanish.

Security and sensitive data

  • The SIN and the bank account numbers (employees and company) are encrypted. They are never sent back to the browser: the screens show only their last 3 characters ("••••••123"). A field left blank keeps the value on file.
  • The full SIN is displayed only on request, on an employee's slip, and this viewing is recorded in the activity log.
  • CSV exports never include the SIN.
  • Forms are protected against request forgery, and pages cannot be displayed in a frame on another site.
  • The activity log keeps, per company, the important events: employee created, payroll set up, pay run confirmed or cancelled, pay stubs sent, direct deposit file generated, remittance payment recorded or cancelled, full SIN displayed. The last 8 entries are on the dashboard.

Screen conventions

  • A red asterisk marks a required field.
  • Numbers accept a comma or a period; spaces, "$" and "%" are ignored. "1,234.56" and "1.234,56" are both understood.
  • The result of an action shows in a window in the middle of the screen: “Done” (green) when it succeeded, “Error” (red) when it failed, with the explanation. “OK”, the Escape key or a click beside it closes it. After a redirect, the message is displayed once on the next page.
  • CSV exports use the semicolon and UTF-8 encoding with BOM: they open directly in Excel. Amounts there use the "0,00" format.
  • The "Print" button uses the browser's printing; the header, the menu and the buttons are not printed.

Settings › Official forms

Config/Formulaires.aspx. Download the year's fillable T4 from the CRA website (t4-fill-YYe.pdf or t4-fill-YYf.pdf) and the fillable RL-1 from Revenu Québec's, then upload them here: "Année des feuillets", "Formulaire" ("T4 (ARC)" or "Relevé 1 (Revenu Québec)"), "Fichier PDF", "Téléverser". The file is checked — it must be a fillable PDF carrying the slip's fields (box 14 of slip 1 for the T4, box A for the RL-1) —, stripped of its encryption and scripts, then kept per company, year and form; a new upload replaces the previous one. "Formulaires en place" table: year, form, file, date, user, size, "Retirer". Uploads and removals are logged. The note under the table says which platform forms are in place (T4, RL-1, T4 instructions): they apply when you have none of your own.

As soon as the year's T4 is in place, that year's slips — employee copy, employer copy, government copies and email attachment (Reports › T4 and RL-1 slips) — come out on the official form: two T4 slips per page, boxes 10, 12, 14, 16/17, 16A/17A, 18, 22, 24, 26, 28, 45, 54, 55, 56, the employer, the employee and their address; boxes 34, 40, 42 and 85 go to "Other information". With the RL-1, each Québec employee gets their page: copy 1 for Revenu Québec, copy 2 for the employee, copy 3 for the employer, boxes A to N and W, slip code "R", identity and address of both parties. The T4 Summary and Summary 1 are still produced by 60secPaie. The forms the platform administration uploads in the Sec60Admin console (Paie › Formulaires T4 et Relevé 1) apply to every company; those you upload here replace them for yours, and the page says which platform forms are in place. Without a form, the slips keep 60secPaie's layout.

T4 instructions (CRA). The platform console also keeps, per year, the CRA's T4 instructions document ("Instructions T4 (ARC)" on the same Paie › Formulaires T4 et Relevé 1 page of Sec60Admin). This document is offered as is to every company: when it is in place for the chosen year, the "Feuillets en PDF et par courriel" card of Reports › T4 and RL-1 slips shows "CRA T4 instructions (year): [file]" and this page lists it, with a link, among the platform forms. The link opens the PDF in a new tab (Documents.aspx?doc=instructions-t4&annee=YYYY, for anyone signed in). It is neither checked beyond its PDF header, nor filled, nor counted in the list of slip years; a company cannot upload its own.

Forms change every year: upload those of the slips' year. The text in the boxes is drawn by 60secPaie inside the PDF itself: it shows identically in every reader. On the T4 and the RL-1, the decimal point of each amount is centred on the form's cents divider (the cents fall in their compartment); on the T4, the small code boxes (10, 29, 45 and the other-information codes) are in 10 points, and the CRA form's "Clear Data" button is removed. The slips produced are read-only: fields are locked and the document is protected against modification, filling and annotation (it opens and prints without a password). Values are written in Courier 9 points, black, as on the slips from common payroll software.

Settings › CNESST units

Config/UnitesCNESST.aspx. The CNESST classifies your business into one or more classification units by activity, each with its own rate in dollars per $100 of insurable wages. This page is for businesses that have several: with a single activity, the company rate is enough and the page can stay empty.

  • List: "Code", "Description", "Rate ($ / $100)", "Employees" (how many are assigned), "Status" (Active or Inactive). Clicking a code opens it for editing.
  • Form "Add a unit" / "Edit unit …": "Unit code" * ("As shown on the classification decision, e.g. 80030."), "Description" *, "Rate ($ per $100 insurable)" * ("The unit's rate for the current year."), "Active" checkbox.
  • A unit used by an employee or a pay run cannot be deleted: "This unit is used by employees or pay runs. Make it inactive instead of deleting it." An employee whose unit is inactive falls back to the company rate.
  • Messages: "Unit {code} already exists.", "The rate seems too high: it is expressed in dollars per $100 of wages.", "Unit {code} saved.", "Unit deleted."

Each employee chooses their unit on their record. At each pay run, the unit's rate is applied and frozen on the pay: the Statement of Wages is then grouped by unit, even if the employee changed unit during the year. Each year, when the new classification decision arrives, update the rates here and the periodic payment rate in Payroll settings.

Understanding the calculations › Formulas, deduction by deduction

Notation: P = periods per year; G = taxable regular pay for the period; B = lump-sum pay (bonus, retro); F = deductions that reduce taxable income; Q = withholdings for labour-sponsored funds. Year-to-date amounts = confirmed pay runs + opening balances.

QPP
Subject if not exempt and aged 18 or over on the pay date. Exemption per period = 3,500 ÷ P, truncated. C = 6.3% × (S3 − exemption), capped at 4,479.30 − year-to-date. QPP2: C2 = 4% × (earnings above $74,600), capped at 416 − year-to-date. Employer share = the employee's. CS (portion deductible from income) = C × 1/6.3 + C2.
Employment Insurance
EI = 1.30% × insurable earnings, capped at 895.70 − year-to-date. Employer = EI × factor (1.4 by default).
QPIP
Employee = 0.43% × earnings, capped at 442.90 − year-to-date. Employer = 0.602% × earnings, capped at 620.06 − year-to-date.
Québec tax (TP-1015.F)
E = line 10 of the TP-1015.3 ($18,952 by default). H = min(6% × G; 1,450 ÷ P). I = P × (G − F − H − CSA) − J − J1, floor 0. Credits = K1 + 14% × E + 15% × P × Q. Y = T × I − K − credits, floor 0. Tax for the period = Y ÷ P, plus the additional withholding. Lump sum: 7% if P × G + previous lump sums + B ≤ $18,952, otherwise the difference in annual tax with and without the lump sum.
Federal tax (T4127, Quebec employee)
TC = TD1 amount ($16,452 by default). A = P × (G − F − CSA) − HD − F1, floor 0. K1 = 14% × TC. K2Q = 14% × [annualized base QPP, capped + annualized EI, capped + annualized QPIP, capped]. K4 = min(14% × A; 14% × 1,501). K3 = other credits. LCF = min(750; 15% × P × Q). T3 = R × A − K − K1 − K2Q − K3 − K4, floor 0. T1 = (T3 − LCF) − 16.5% × T3: this is the Québec abatement. Tax for the period = T1 ÷ P, plus the TD1 additional tax. Lump sum: 10% if the $5,000 threshold is not exceeded, otherwise the difference method.
HSF
HSF earnings × HSF rate ÷ 100. No HSF for an exempt employee.
CNESST and CNT
Earnings = min(earnings; 103,000 − year-to-date). CNESST = earnings × rate ÷ 100. CNT = earnings × 0.06%. The CNESST exclusion on the record removes both.
Vacation
Accrued = vacationable earnings × rate ÷ 100. It is an accrual: it is not paid out. "Vacation pay" lines reduce the balance.
Net pay
Gross paid − (federal tax + Québec tax + QPP + QPP2 + EI + QPIP + other deductions).

Rounding

  • To the cent, half a cent rounded up.
  • The QPP exemption per period is truncated, not rounded.
  • Amount E (Québec personal credits) is rounded to the dollar.
  • Line amount = hours × rate × multiplier, rounded to the cent.
  • All intermediate values have a floor of 0.

Formulas

  • CPP: 5.95% × (earnings for the period – $3,500 ÷ number of periods), up to the year's maximum. The employer pays the same amount. Contributions run from the first pay dated in the month after the 18th birthday to the last pay dated in the month of the 70th birthday.
  • Employment insurance: 1.63% of insurable earnings, up to the year's maximum.
  • Annual taxable income: the same for both taxes — remuneration for the period less the deductions that reduce tax (RPP, RRSP, union dues) and the enhanced part of the CPP contribution, multiplied by the number of periods.
  • Federal tax: federal table less credits (TD1 claim amount, CPP and EI contributions, Canada employment amount), with no abatement, divided by the number of periods.
  • Ontario tax: Ontario table less credits (TD1ON claim amount, CPP and EI contributions), plus the surtax, plus the health premium, less the tax reduction; all divided by the number of periods. The health premium is part of the Ontario tax withheld: it has no separate line on the pay stub.
  • Bonus and retroactive pay: the tax is the difference between the year's tax with and without the payment. When the year's remuneration does not exceed $5,000, the deduction is 15% in all: 10% federal and 5% Ontario.
  • EHT: remuneration for the pay × effective rate. The effective rate is the table rate spread over the whole payroll once the exemption is subtracted; it is zero when the estimated payroll does not exceed the exemption.
  • WSIB: insurable earnings × rate of the employee's class (otherwise the company's) ÷ 100, up to the year's ceiling.
  • Vacation: vacation pay accrues on gross wages, excluding vacation pay itself.
The detail of each pay (step 3 of the wizard, "Details and calculation check") shows the intermediate values: PI, F5, A, K1, K2, T1 for federal; T4, V1 (surtax), V2 (health premium), S (reduction) and T2 for Ontario.

Remittances

In Ontario, everything is remitted to the Receiver General for Canada, in a single payment: "Federal tax", "Ontario tax", "CPP - employee contributions", "CPP - employer contribution", "Employment insurance - employee premiums" and "Employment insurance - employer premium". There is no remittance to Revenu Québec: that choice is not offered. An "Ontario: amounts outside the remittances" card gives the year-to-date Employer Health Tax and WSIB premium: "These amounts are paid separately, to the provincial or territorial administration and to its workers' compensation board. They are not part of the remittances to the Receiver General."

Reports

  • "T4 slips": boxes 16 and 16A for CPP (instead of 17 and 17A), box 10 "ON", box 22 combining federal tax and Ontario tax. No boxes 55 and 56, no RL-1, no RL-1 summary. An "Ontario: amounts outside the remittances" card gives the remuneration subject to EHT and the calculated amounts.
  • "WSIB insurable earnings": the CNESST report, for the WSIB — gross wages, amount over the ceiling, insurable earnings and premium by employee, by class and by month.
  • "Journal entries": same accounts, Ontario labels ("CPP payable", "EHT payable", "WSIB payable"…). EHT and WSIB are recorded as expenses and as amounts payable.

Limits

  • An employee paid in Québec and in Ontario in the same year appears on a single slip, with a warning: the CRA requires one T4 per province, and the split must be done by hand.
  • The Ontario labour-sponsored fund tax credit is not calculated.
  • The employer's share of a private health insurance plan is a taxable benefit in Québec only: it cannot be added to an Ontario pay ("This benefit is taxable in Québec only: it cannot be added to a pay of another province.") and is not copied from the employee's recurring items.
  • EHT rates and the WSIB ceiling should be confirmed with the Ontario Ministry of Finance and the WSIB. Compare a few pays with the CRA's online calculator (PDOC).

Understanding the calculations › Payroll for the other provinces and territories

60secPaie calculates payroll for all thirteen provinces and territories of Canada. The "Province of employment" field under Settings › Payroll settings offers them all: Québec, Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia, Prince Edward Island, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Nunavut, Saskatchewan, Newfoundland and Labrador, Northwest Territories and Yukon. As for Ontario, it is the province of the establishment where employees report for work; a company has one province, and each pay keeps its own. What is common outside Québec

  • CPP instead of QPP, with its second additional contribution; the employer pays the same amount.
  • Employment insurance at the regular rate; there is no QPIP and no labour standards contribution (CNT).
  • Federal tax without the abatement, and the tax of the province or territory calculated under the CRA's T4127 guide, on the same taxable income.
  • Everything is remitted to the Receiver General for Canada, in a single payment: federal tax, provincial tax, CPP and employment insurance. There is no remittance to Revenu Québec.
  • T4 only: box 10 = province code, boxes 16 and 16A for CPP, box 22 combining federal tax and provincial tax. No RL-1, no RL-1 summary.
  • Vacation pay accrues on gross wages, excluding vacation pay itself, at the vacation rate entered.

The provincial and territorial tax rates come from the CRA's T4127 guide, "Payroll Deductions Formulas": the 122nd edition, effective January 1, 2026, and the 123rd edition, effective July 1, 2026. Each set of rates carries its effective date, and a pay uses the one that applies on its date. They are kept up to date in Sec60Admin (Payroll › Rates for the year), with no software update. As long as a province's rates are not defined for a year, payroll is refused there: "The … rates are not yet defined in the software for the province". Practical case › First start-up

  1. Sign in with your 60Sec account. You are sent to Settings › Payroll settings.
  2. Fill in the default frequency, the vacation rate, the RP and RS numbers, the remittance frequency, the estimated payroll, the HSF sector, the EI factor and the CNESST rate. "Save" creates six basic pay items.
  3. In Settings › Pay items, add what you are missing: commissions, RRSP, group insurance, union dues…
  4. In Settings › Chart of accounts, enter your account numbers if you want the journal entries report.
  5. In Settings › Direct deposit, enter the settings provided by your bank, if you pay by direct deposit.
  6. In Employees, create or check each employee, then open "Payroll settings": SIN, frequency, rate or salary, TD1 and TP-1015.3, direct deposit, pay stub by email. Save, then define their recurring items.
  7. If you are starting mid-year, enter the opening balances.
  8. Run a first pay run and compare it with your previous system or with the CRA's and Revenu Québec's calculators before confirming it.

Practical case › Mid-year conversion

For each employee, in Employees › Opening balances, enter the year's cumulative amounts taken from your previous system: gross, taxes withheld, QPP pensionable earnings and contributions (base and 2nd additional), EI, QPIP employee and employer, CNESST insurable wages, vacation balance. Without them, 60secPaie would keep withholding QPP, EI and QPIP past the maximums, and the slips would be incomplete. Remittances already made in the previous system are not entered in 60secPaie: its summaries cover only the pay runs it confirmed, adjust the year-end summaries accordingly.

Practical case › Hiring someone

  1. Employees › New employee: identity and contact details. They appear in 60Sec right away.
  2. On their record: SIN (ask for it, it is mandatory for the slips), date of birth, language, frequency if it differs from the company's, hourly rate and hours per week or annual salary, TD1 and TP-1015.3 provided by the employee (leave blank for the basic amounts), direct deposit and email.
  3. Pay items: add their wages if you want a template, and their recurring deductions.
  4. They are included in the next pay run whose period end is after their hire date.

Practical case › Employee departure

  1. Run their last pay normally, with a "Vacation pay" line to pay out their vacation balance (visible on their stub, "Vacation balance").
  2. In 60Sec, enter their termination date or make them inactive. They are no longer included in pay runs whose period start is after their termination date.
  3. The Record of Employment (ROE) is produced in Service Canada's ROE Web; 60secPaie does not produce it. Their T4 and RL-1 slips come out at year end like everyone else's.

The check report shows "Tax on lump-sum payment" separately. QPP, EI and QPIP apply normally, up to the maximums.

Practical case › Vacation

  • Accrual: each pay accrues the vacation rate (4% or 6%) on eligible earnings. It is an accrual visible on the stub ("Vacation accrued", "Vacation balance") and in the journal entries ("Vacation expense" / "Vacation payable").
  • Vacation taken: add a "Vacation pay" line with the amount paid. It reduces the balance and is posted against "Vacation payable".
  • Vacation paid each pay (part-time or temporary employees): use "Vacation pay paid each pay period"; this line does not accrue vacation on itself.
  • An opening balance is entered in the opening balances.

Practical case › Annual payroll

For an owner who pays themselves a salary once a year: on their record, "Pay period" = "Annual (1 period)", and their annual salary. In the wizard, choose "Annual (1 period)"; the period runs from January 1 to the chosen end. Deductions are calculated on the whole year, with the full $3,500 QPP exemption. The wizard does not mix frequencies: biweekly employees and the annual employee are handled in separate pay runs.

Practical case › Correcting a pay run

  1. If the pay run is still a draft: go back to step 2, edit the lines, recalculate.
  2. If it is confirmed and it is the most recent: open its details and "Cancel pay run". If its deductions have already been remitted, cancel that payment first in Remittances › History. Then redo the pay run. Cheques already issued keep their number; new ones get a new one.
  3. If an older pay run is involved: cancel the subsequent pay runs one by one, from the most recent to the oldest, or correct with an adjustment line ("Retroactive pay" or a deduction) in the next pay run. The year-to-date amounts and the slips will reflect the correction.

Practical case › First direct deposit

  1. Sign up for your bank's automated funds transfer service; it gives you an originator ID, a data centre and a channel for submitting files.
  2. Enter these settings and the company account in Settings › Direct deposit.
  3. On each employee's record, check "This employee is paid by direct deposit" and enter transit, institution and account (on a void cheque: 5-digit transit, 3-digit institution, then the account).
  4. Confirm a pay run, then "Download the direct deposit file" from its details. Have this first file validated by the bank (test file) before a real deposit.
  5. Submit the file to the bank before the pay date, according to its lead time.

Practical case › Year end

Year end happens in January and February: close the payroll year, produce the T4 and RL-1 slips, file them with the governments and hand them to the employees, then report wages to the CNESST. Everything comes from the confirmed pay runs and the opening balances; nothing is typed by hand into the slips.

  3. Check the employee records (Employees › Record): SIN present, full address, province of employment, box 45 (dental care), CPP/QPP, EI and QPIP exemptions and, for emailing the slips, the email address with the "Send the pay stub by email" box. Each download is logged, the SIN being complete.
  7. Check the boxes against the year's RC4120 (T4) and RL-1.G (RL-1) guides.
  8. Report wages to the CNESST in Mon Espace CNESST before March 15, with Reports › CNESST statement of wages; the "Reconciliation" card gives the difference with your payments.
  9. Prepare the following year. The following year's rates must be validated by 60Sec in its administration console before the first January pay run; otherwise the wizard refuses the pay run ("The government rates for {year} are not yet defined in the software."). The PDF slips produced are read-only: a correction is made at the source (pay run, opening balances, record), then the slip is produced again and sent again ("also resend slips already sent", or the row's "Courriel" button). Reference › Frequently asked questions

An employee does not appear in the pay run. Why?

Four conditions: they are active in 60Sec; their payroll is "Set up" (record saved at least once); their frequency (their own, otherwise the company's) is that of the run; their hire date is no later than the period end and their termination date is blank or after the period start. Also check that they have lines: without a template, rate or salary, they appear with "No lines".

An employee's QPP is 0.

They are under 18 on the pay date, or "Do not contribute to the QPP" is checked, or their annual maximum ($4,479.30 plus $416 of QPP2) is reached, opening balances included, or their pay is below the per-period exemption ($3,500 ÷ periods).

Why is federal tax lower than in another province?

Quebec employees benefit from the 16.5% Québec abatement on federal tax, and their EI rate is reduced (1.30%) because they contribute to the QPIP.

Can I have two drafts at the same time?

No. Only one draft per company. Finish it or delete it before starting another. Two frequencies (for example biweekly and monthly) are therefore paid in two successive runs.

How do I change an employee's name or address?

In 60Sec. 60secPaie only writes there to create an employee; the rest of the identity is edited in 60Sec and is reflected here immediately.

The SIN shows "••••••123". Where is the rest?

Encrypted. It is never sent back to the browser. On an employee's slip, "Show full SIN" displays it, and this viewing is logged.

The suggested remittance amount does not match my CRA statement.

60secPaie only knows the pay runs it confirmed. Pay runs done elsewhere or before the conversion are not in its remittances. The configured remittance frequency must also match the one the CRA assigned to you.

The HSF on the RL-1 Summary differs from what Revenu Québec calculates.

The rate applied during the year comes from the estimated payroll in Settings. The actual rate is calculated on the year's payroll; the difference is settled in the RL-1 Summary, as the report's note indicates.

I want to print all the pay stubs of a pay run.

Open the pay run details and the "Pay stub" link for each employee; each stub has "Print pay stub" and "Download as PDF". For a group send, use "Send pay stubs by email".

What does "Vacation accrued" mean on the stub?

The vacation accrual earned in this pay (rate × eligible earnings). It is not paid out; it is added to the "Vacation balance", which is drawn down by "Vacation pay" lines.

Does a hidden item line count?

Yes. "Do not show on the pay stub" only changes the display: the line is calculated, taxed and posted normally.

Can I pay a supplier or a self-employed worker?

No. 60secPaie only handles employees within the meaning of tax laws, with source deductions. A self-employed worker is paid by invoice, in 60Sec.

How do the 2027 rates get into the software?

Each year's rates (T4127, TP-1015.F, QPP, EI, QPIP, HSF, CNESST) are entered and validated by 60Sec in its administration console as soon as the guides are published, in December. You have nothing to do: once validated, 60secPaie uses them for any pay run dated in that year, within five minutes. Until a year is validated, the wizard refuses a pay run dated in that year.

I have several CNESST classification units. What do I do?

Enter each unit and its rate under Settings › CNESST units, then pick each employee's unit on their record. The unit's rate applies to their pay runs, and the CNESST report gives a table per unit, ready for the Statement of Wages. Employees without a unit stay at the company rate.

My slips come out on 60secPaie's layout rather than on the CRA or Revenu Québec form.

No official form is in place for the year: upload the CRA's fillable T4 and Revenu Québec's fillable RL-1 under Settings › Official forms, or wait for the ones the platform administration uploads in its console. The note under the "Feuillets en PDF et par courriel" card says which ones are in place.

Where are the CRA's T4 instructions?

Once the platform administration has uploaded the year's document, the "CRA T4 instructions (year)" link appears under Reports › T4 and RL-1 slips for the chosen year, and under Settings › Official forms among the platform forms. The PDF opens in a new tab.

Can I correct a slip in the PDF produced?

No: the slips produced are read-only (locked fields, protected document). Fix the source — pay run, opening balances, employee record, box 45 or exemptions — then produce the slip again; the email can be sent again with "also resend slips already sent" or the row's "Courriel" button.

Which copy of the slips should I download: employer or employee?

"Copy to download" drives the "Download all T4 slips" and "Download all RL-1 slips" buttons and each row's "T4" and "Relevé 1" buttons. The employer copy is the one to file with the summaries; the employee copy is the one they receive, normally by email ("Email the slips" button). The government copies, with the summaries, have their own button and do not depend on this choice. On the official form, the CRA's T4 is the same for every copy; the RL-1 has one page per copy (1 Revenu Québec, 2 employee, 3 employer).

An employee did not receive their slip by email.

The bulk send only serves employees whose record ticks "Send the pay stub by email" and carries a valid address; the tally names those skipped. An employee already served for the year is not sent again without the "also resend slips already sent" box. For a single employee, the "Courriel" button on their row resends their slips even if already sent; without an address, download their T4 and RL-1 with the row's buttons and hand them the PDF or a printout. The date of the last send shows under the "Feuillet" link.

The year of my slips is not offered in the list.

The list offers the years that have at least one confirmed pay run, opening balances or an uploaded official T4 or RL-1 form (yours or the platform's). A T4 instructions document alone does not create a year. A pay run dated in January belongs to the following year, even for a December period.

Do last year's official forms serve this year?

No: each form is kept per year and serves only that year's slips. Download the new year's fillable T4 and fillable RL-1 from the CRA and Revenu Québec websites, then upload them under Settings › Official forms, unless the platform administration already did it for everyone; the note under the slips card says which ones are in place. Yours take precedence over the platform's.

Why is the download of slips recorded in the activity log?

Because the complete SIN is in it, as on the official slip. Each download, for one employee or for all, and the display of the complete SIN on a slip's page leave a trace with the date and the user. The CSV, for its part, does not export the SIN.

Does 60secPaie file my T4 and RL-1 slips with the CRA and Revenu Québec?

No. It calculates the boxes, produces the slips and summaries, and sends them to the employees; filing is done in Web Forms (CRA) and My Account for businesses (Revenu Québec), from the government copies, before the last day of February. See Practical case › Year end.

My PDF reader shows the slip's boxes empty.

Slips produced now carry the text of each box drawn inside the PDF itself: it shows identically in every reader. A file produced before this version let the reader draw the text, which some do not; produce the slip again.
